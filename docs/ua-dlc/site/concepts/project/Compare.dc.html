Three Ways HyperDelivery Could Look

The same projects, people, approvals and facts in all three. Only how you move around changes.

Pages A, B and C draw the same seven screens in the same places: Home, Inbox, Projects, New Project, Project, Approval and Handoff. Page B also holds today's other boards.

A · Top Bar

Pages and tabs under a header. Home is a dashboard of what needs you; each project is a page with tabs for its brief, mockup, plan and prototype, and the conversation in a panel at the right.

Feels Like

Jira, ServiceNow, SharePoint

Best For

People in it every day who track several projects at once: owners, approvers and leads.

Pros

  • Familiar to anyone who uses UA's enterprise tools.
  • Shows a lot at once: lists, tables and tabs across many projects.
  • Every page has a fixed place you can bookmark and share.
  • Library, Portfolio and Admin fit as more tabs.

Cons

  • More to learn up front: tabs, panels and menus.
  • The AI sits in a side panel and feels like one feature among many.
  • Busy for someone who signs one thing a month.
  • The top bar gets crowded as screens are added.

B · Side Nav and Chat

A side nav on the left and a conversation at the center. Home asks what you want to make; each project is a conversation beside a panel holding its brief, mockup, plan and prototype.

Feels Like

ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot

Best For

People who start by describing a need and like to talk the work through, and anyone at home in ChatGPT or Copilot.

Pros

  • Works like the AI tools people already use.
  • Starting is as easy as typing what you need.
  • The conversation and what it made share one screen.
  • Recent projects are one click away, each marked when it waits on you.

Cons

  • A blank box can stall people who don't know what to ask.
  • Decisions can get lost in a long conversation.
  • Two panes side by side leave less room for big tables and the prototype.
  • It looks like Copilot, so people may ask why not just use Copilot.

C · Guided Path

The UA-DLC line is the navigation. Home shows the one next thing waiting on you; each project walks its stations step by step, with the assistant on call in a drawer.

Feels Like

TurboTax, DocuSign

Best For

People who come in now and then: a business owner who signs a few approvals a month, or a first-time requester.

Pros

  • Nothing to learn: the next step is always obvious.
  • Signing feels deliberate: see what changed, try it, check the evidence, then sign.
  • Projects sit in a column for their station, so the board reads like the line.
  • What waits on you leads Home, one item at a time.

Cons

  • Slower for people who use it every day.
  • The AI sits in a drawer, so it is less visible and can feel less smart.
  • Jumping between projects or steps takes more clicks.
  • The most to build: each station needs its own guided flow.
